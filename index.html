The thesis

Why Tier 3 and Tier 4 cities

Institutional capital in Indian housing has concentrated in the metros. We think the more durable opportunity is further out.

  1. Buyers who mean to live there

    We build for people who will live in the house: families moving out of rented or joint homes, professionals coming back from the metros. That demand builds slowly, and it does not vanish when sentiment turns.

  2. The gap is in supply, not demand

    New housing in these cities is mostly built plot by plot, or by small local builders. A registered project with clear title, a fixed specification and an honest handover date is still uncommon. That is the gap we build into.

  3. Returns from execution, not land

    Land is a smaller share of total cost than in a metro, so a project's return depends on what we control: design, construction cost and time. We underwrite on those and assume no land appreciation.

  4. Phases we can finish

    We size each project to what we can build, sell and hand over well. Capital is committed in phases, and each phase has to stand on its own numbers.

The firm

Women-led, and built on the ground

DuRGA Capital is a women-led firm. The name comes from ‘durg’, the fort: the ground a thing is built on. That is how we approach the work. Clear title, sound approvals and honest timelines come before anything else.

We would rather build fewer homes well than many quickly, and we would rather show one project done carefully than a long list.
